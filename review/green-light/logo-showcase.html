Good Trouble — Logo Exploration v1

Ten concepts, five palette families, zero preconditions. Every mark answers the same brief: a two-person company selling an adaptive AI platform, named for productive disruption — order deliberately, precisely broken. Black/orange appears only where continuity is the point (#03, #08).

01

The Disruptor Dot

ink / coral

A perfect grid with one dot deliberately out of line — bigger, brighter, escaping the pattern. That IS good trouble: everything orderly except the one element breaking rank on purpose. Also reads as the platform: eight standard modules, one adapting to the owner. Scales to a 16px favicon without losing the story.

02

Open Loop

black / gold

A heavy circle that refuses to close, with a gold spark sitting exactly where the seam should be. The broken ring says the system is never 'done' — we keep building — and the gap is the trouble: a deliberate interruption of the perfect form. Premium black/gold pulls from the brand-exploration direction already on file and looks at home on a med spa's front desk.

03

GT Ligature

signal

G and T fused into one mark — but the T physically breaks out of the G's boundary instead of sitting politely inside it. The letterform commits the trespass. Signal orange kept here on purpose: this is the 'continuity' candidate if you want a new mark without a full rebrand.

04

Shapeshift

slate / cyan

Half square, half circle — one solid shape caught mid-transformation, with a cyan point marking the leading edge. This is the product truth drawn literally: rigid software on the left becoming something that reshapes around the owner on the right. The differentiator (adaptability, not breadth) as a single silhouette.

05

Flatline Break

midnight / lime

A flat line that erupts into a pulse and settles on a higher baseline. The before/after of every client: dormant list, silent front desk, flat months — then signal. The line ends higher than it started and the lime dot is the live edge, still transmitting. Also the most honest nod to what the platform actually does: it finds the signal.

06

Tilt

cream / oxblood

A solid block rotated seven degrees out of its assigned frame — the ghost outline shows where it was 'supposed' to be. Quietest concept in the set: defiance with total composure. Cream and oxblood photograph beautifully in aesthetic/wellness spaces, which matters when the logo lives on med spa proposals and gift-drop cards.

07

Ripple

ink / coral

One small point of impact, expanding waves — asymmetric, moving up and out. Two people in Florida making waves disproportionate to their size; one automated touch rippling through a client base. The arcs only extend one direction, which gives the mark momentum instead of the static bullseye every other 'ripple' logo settles for.

08

Wordmark — Baseline Break

signal

Pure type, stacked and heavy, with a single letter knocked below the baseline in accent color. The word behaves the way the brand does: 95% discipline, one precise violation. Works everywhere a symbol can't — email signatures, invoices, the site header — and the dropped B becomes a repeatable brand gesture (drop one element in any layout).

09

Hub & Drift

black / gold

A hub with three solid connections and one gold node still being reached for — dashed line, not yet solid. The platform as a living system: modules online, and always one more coming. The dashed edge does the 'locked modules / we keep building' story visually, which no other concept in this set even attempts.

10

The Wink

midnight / lime

Two bars: one standing straight, one leaning — the second in acid lime, clearly enjoying itself. Reads as an abstract 'GT', a wink, or a pair where one partner is up to something. The most personality-forward mark in the set: friendly enough for a beauty-industry audience, sharp enough for a software company, and drawable from memory after one glance — the real test of a logo.
