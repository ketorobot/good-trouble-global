The 30-Second Pitch

Memorize it, then say it like you didn't.

"I'm Augie — co-founder of a two-person Miami software company. We build a platform for med spas that answers the phone at 9pm, follows up with clients who went quiet, and sends unhappy reviews to you instead of Google. I'm not an agency, and there's no presentation. I'd like to build you a free demo with your spa's name and services on it — takes us two days, costs nothing. Who should I send it to?"

The Four Rules of the Walk-In

Never pitch in sentence one. Lead with a question about their day, not your product. The second they smell a pitch, you're a vendor. Vendors wait in the lobby forever.
The front desk is the client, not the obstacle. They feel the missed calls and the 9pm DMs more than the owner does. Win them and they sell you upstairs.
Ten seconds if they're slammed. "Totally get it — name and I'm gone in ten seconds." Then be gone in ten seconds. That restraint is the impression.
The ask is a NAME, not a sale. You win the visit when you leave with the owner's first name and a number to text. Everything else is bonus.

Same-Day Follow-Up (non-negotiable)

Text within 4 hours, while they remember your face: "Augie from Good Trouble — stopped by [Spa] today. Here's the demo I mentioned: goodtroubleglobal.com/demo/medspa.html. Want one with [Spa]'s name on it? Two days, free, no strings."

Opening Lines

Eight that get past a front desk without being sleazy. Pick by vibe, not order.

1"Who's the sharpest person here on the systems side?"
2"Got two minutes for the owner before I bounce?"
3"I build the kind of thing that makes front desk easier — mind if I drop it with whoever runs that?"
4"Quick favor: who decides how follow-ups and bookings run around here?"
5"I'm local and I only need the owner for sixty seconds — can you point me?"
6"Need the person who actually feels the no-shows and the review headaches."
7"I don't sell services, just a tool — owner around, or should I leave it?"
8"Short question for whoever's tired of chasing clients — where's that person?"

The Five Ways a Walk-In Dies (and the Dodge)

"We already have software." → "Cool — I'm not here to rip anything out. Just seeing if there's one gap worth plugging."
"Owner's never here." → "No worries — give me their name and I'll shoot a two-line text so they decide if it's worth the meet."
They clock the pitch in sentence one. → You broke Rule One. Reset: ask about their day.
They're slammed. → "Totally get it — name and I'm gone in ten seconds."
They treat you like a vendor. → "I'm not selling ads or services — just a Miami-built thing that sits on top of what you already do."

Getting the Owner's Contact When Only Staff Is Present

A"Who's the owner, so I can text them one line and be out of your hair?"
B"What's the owner's first name? I'll handle the rest."
C"Drop their name and number real quick — I'll keep it to a single text."
D"Who owns the place? I just need the name so they can ignore me or not."

Objection Battlecard

What they say → what they're actually afraid of → what you say back. Honest counters only — we're pre-first-client and we don't pretend otherwise.

1. "We already have software that does most of this."
Afraid of: rip-out chaos and redundant spend.
"Keep it. This sits on top and catches what falls through — the 9pm DMs, the calls that ring during treatments, the follow-ups nobody got to. If it catches nothing, you've lost nothing."
2. "AI will sound robotic to my clients."
Afraid of: brand damage in a business built on personal touch.
"Fair — most of it does. Click the demo and read the actual conversations it has. If any line makes you cringe, show me the line and I'll show you how we'd tune it to your spa's voice. That tuning is the whole product."
3. "You don't have any clients yet."
Afraid of: being the guinea pig.
"Right — you'd be first, and I won't pretend otherwise. First means two founders building the platform around YOUR spa, and founding-client terms that don't exist once there's a logo wall. Somebody gets that deal. I'm asking if it should be you."
4. "What happens to my data if you fold?"
Afraid of: hostage client lists.
"It's your data — full export, any time, in writing. If we vanish tomorrow, you keep every client record, every list, every review. We'll put that in the agreement before you sign anything."
5. "My front desk handles this."
Afraid of: you implying her people are the problem.
"And she's probably great. This isn't for the calls she answers — it's for the ones that ring while she's checking someone in, and the DMs at 9pm when she's home. She gets backup, not competition."
6. "Is it HIPAA compliant?"
Afraid of: liability landing on their license.
"Right question. Instead of a yes-checkbox, we'll walk you through exactly how data is stored, what's encrypted, and what we sign. If your compliance person wants to grill us, put them on the phone — we like that meeting."
7. "How much is it?"
Afraid of: a number they can't unhear.
"Monthly subscription, no long-term contract. Numbers come after you've seen your own demo — until you've seen what it does for your spa, any number is noise."
8. "I'm locked into my current system's contract."
Afraid of: paying twice.
"When's it up? The personalized demo is free and takes two days. Have it in your pocket before renewal and decide with something real in hand."
9. "I tried a tool like this before. It didn't work."
Afraid of: getting burned twice.
"Most tools make you learn them, then never change. Ours reshapes around what you care about — that's the category difference. Your last tool is the reason we built this one."
10. "My clients want human interaction."
Afraid of: the spa losing its soul.
"So do we. The platform takes the 2am booking and the follow-up nag — the human stuff stays human. It buys your people time to actually give it."
11. "I don't have time for this."
Afraid of: one more thing to manage.
"You just made the pitch for me. Sixty seconds for the link — the demo's yours to click at midnight when the day's finally done."
12. "Just send me some information."
Not afraid — brushing you off.
"I'll do better. Give me the spa's name and I'll send a demo built for it — your services, your branding, two days. Information gets deleted. Your own name on a dashboard gets clicked."

After the Walk

The Cadence

Same day: the text (page 2). Demo link + offer of a personalized build.
Day 3: if no reply — one line: "Built one of these for a spa like yours this week — want yours before Friday?"
Day 7: last touch, then rotate them to the email track: "No stress either way — the demo stays live if the timing changes."
Reply = DEMO build. Anyone who says yes: name + services to Braddock/Claude same day; personalized demo ships within 2 business days. That promise is the product's first impression — never miss it.

Scorekeeping

After each door, 30 seconds in your phone: spa, who you talked to, owner name (got it? Y/N), vibe 1–5, next step. That note is what turns a walk into a pipeline.

What "Winning" Looks Like

Ten doors → six owner names → four demos sent → one walkthrough call. That's a good week for a company at our stage. The first signed client is the only milestone that matters — and it's most likely standing behind one of these 22 counters.
