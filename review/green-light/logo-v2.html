Good Trouble — Logo v2: One Letter Off

Braddock's call from v1: concept #8 (Wordmark — Baseline Break) is the direction. No abstract marks. These are ten variations of the same idea — the full company name in heavy type, disciplined everywhere except one letter making good trouble. Every one works in email signatures, invoices, and the site header at any size.

08.0 — THE ORIGINAL

Baseline Break (v1 #8, unchanged)

The reference point. Stacked, heavy, orange B dropped 5 units. Everything below is a riff on this.

08.1

Baseline Break — Dark

Same mark on brand black. This is how it reads in the site header and on the demo app. The orange B pops harder on dark.

08.2

One Line — Bebas

Single line in Bebas Neue, the font the whole site already runs on. Zero new fonts, total brand consistency, same dropped B. The cheapest one to adopt today.

08.3

The Raised O

Flip the gesture: the troublemaker is in GOOD, popped above the line. Reads as a wink — even the "good" half has one loose screw.

08.4

Last Letter Loose

The final E in orange — the word ends by breaking rank, like the trouble starts where the name ends. (Static color shift only; a tilted-E cut needs the type outlined in the final asset.)

08.5

One Line — Heavy

Horizontal lockup in the heavy cut for wide placements: email signature, invoice header, browser tab + name. Same B, smaller drop so it holds at small sizes.

08.6

Black B, Orange Floor

The B stays black but lands on an orange platform — the drop leaves a mark. Subtler; survives one-color printing better than any other version (the bar just becomes a rule).

08.7

The Dropped O

Same gesture, rounder letter. The O drop reads cleaner at favicon size than the B (simpler shape) — worth testing both at 16px before locking.

08.8

Baseline Break — Black/Gold

The same mark in the premium black/gold palette from the earlier brand exploration. For proposals and anything that sits on a med spa's front desk.

08.9

GT Monogram + Lockup

The one-letter-off gesture compressed to two letters for the favicon and app icon, with the full name beside it. The dropped T is the same move at icon scale — the wordmark and the icon tell one story.
